## What the new labels mean

- **Numbered source summary:** a short editorial paraphrase of a checked numbered passage or explicit numbered discussion in an author article.
- **Related teaching:** the author discusses the theme; connecting it to this sutra is our editorial work. No direct sutra comment is claimed.
- **Reported author teaching:** another writer explicitly attributes the teaching or quotation to the selected author. The surrounding writer’s own commentary is excluded.
- Modern-author summaries are concise study notes, not full reproductions or substitutes for the copyrighted books.

## Reference audit

- The shared study index remains 196: 51 / 55 / 56 / 34. Cite each source’s own number.
- The Rāma Prasāda online chapter three ends at 3.54. Source 3.1–3.19 match the same study numbers; study 3.20 appears in prose under source 3.19. - Online Rāma Prasāda 1.39 has an incorrect Sanskrit block repeating 1.33. Its word gloss and commentary support the 1.39 placement. The original project Sanskrit is retained.
- Bryant’s 2020 article uses source 3.24 / 3.25 / 3.37 / 3.42 for study 3.25 / 3.26 / 3.38 / 3.43. Its article-specific numbers are retained; a complete book crosswalk is not inferred.
- Iyengar I.41 was checked in YogAfrica June 2024, printed pages 9–10. Page 9 quotes Patanjala Yoga Sutra Paricaya; the added summary uses the Light on the Yoga Sutras excerpt on page 10. Other selected Iyengar entries were read in a third-party book OCR; full scan collation remains pending.
- A secondary Iyengar obstacles blog contained purported quotations that could not be confirmed as exact wording in the consulted book text. Those quotations were excluded.
- Integral Yoga articles written by disciples are not wholesale attributed to Satchidananda. Only explicitly attributed teachings are summarized and marked as reported.

## Remaining gaps and limits

- Sidersky: nine existing interview connections remain. Further English and Russian searches did not yield a verifiable complete numbered commentary. This is a search result, not proof that no unpublished or unavailable work exists.
- Vivekananda: 174 separate prose entries and 195 translations remain. Some entries in the consulted transcription give only a translation; these are not filled with invented prose.
- Taimni, Satchidananda, Iyengar and Bryant have book-length commentaries, but this supplement adds only selected checked passages and author articles. It does not claim full modern-author coverage.
- Historical prose is an online transcription; OCR errors and editorial additions may remain. It is not yet a fully proofread critical edition.
- Descriptions of siddhis report classical metaphysical explanations and do not establish extraordinary powers experimentally.
- Each new modern note has its source, location, attribution type and original number where verified. A machine-readable supplement includes all additions and the historical crosswalk.
- Rights restrictions already recorded in the foundation still apply, including CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 for the GRETIL-derived Sanskrit.
