# Sources, numbering and editorial notes

## Status and reuse

This package is a research collection, not a new critical edition or an exhaustive collection of every commentary. Sanskrit and English passages come from named sources. No new English translation is presented as authoritative.

The ancient Sanskrit work and historical English originals are public-domain works. **The particular GRETIL e-text is distributed under [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/).** Retain its attribution and license for the Sanskrit extracted here and the generated Devanagari adaptation. Changes: whitespace normalization, extraction into records, project renumbering and conversion from IAST to Devanagari; 3.22 is supplemented from a separately identified historical source. No source endorsement is implied.

For a commercial book or app, resolve use of this e-text before publication—for example, obtain permission or make and proofread an independent transcription from a suitable public-domain printed edition. Do not treat this whole research package as unrestricted commercial content. The historical English text, our editorial notes and modern websites are distinct layers; no rights are claimed over underlying public-domain originals. The modern Sidersky video has not been reproduced here.

## Editorial limitations

- This is a comparison of historical translations, not a literal word-by-word grammatical analysis. The sources can reflect different Sanskrit readings and philosophical assumptions.
- Generated Devanagari follows the GRETIL IAST rather than a second independently proofread manuscript. It retains the source’s word divisions; avagraha marks represent elision. No chanting accents are supplied.
- Counts, identifier sequences and the two numbering shifts were checked. The complete online commentaries were collected, but every sentence has not been collated against printed pages. Do not describe this as a publication-ready proofread edition.
- Online transcription errors were found. Exact pre-cleanup English strings are retained in the main JSON for Vivekananda and Woods. The changes below are explicit editorial changes, not invisible replacement of a translator’s interpretation.
- Woods 2.20 prints “Buddha” where the Sanskrit supplied here has *śuddha*. That suspicious word is retained pending scan comparison. Historical terminology such as “mind-stuff,” “psychic,” and “Isolation” has generally been preserved.
- The Project Gutenberg Johnston transcription was inspected but not used for the delivered text after apparent corruptions were found. The Theosophical Library transcription is the delivered source.
- Sanskrit sandhi, compound division, punctuation and variant readings still deserve specialist review before a final book. Modern commentator claims also need comparison with the specific sutras and their context.
