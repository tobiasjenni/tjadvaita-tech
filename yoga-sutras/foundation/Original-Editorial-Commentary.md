# Original editorial commentary on all 196 Yoga Sutras

Version 0.3 · 15 September 2026

196 entries, using the combined study index. **Citations follow the original source edition and number.** The study number is a navigation aid. Sanskrit and translation references are listed at every entry.

These are original AI-generated readings and assessments by Codex, written for this project. “I” expresses an analytical position, not lived spiritual experience. This is a draft for human editorial and Sanskrit review. The comments are not attributed to Patañjali or any selected commentator.

The position taken here is sympathetic to contemplative inquiry, attentive to ethical consequences, and explicit about the difference between historical doctrine, modern application and claims needing evidence. It does not assume that every commentator agrees or that modern adaptations exhaust classical Yoga.

The immediate textual basis is the collected Sanskrit and historical English translations. Modern copyrighted books have not been turned into complete per-sutra summaries. See [the audit](Collection-Audit.md) for coverage and limits.

## Study index 1.2

**Reading:** Yoga is defined through nirodha of the mind’s activities. “Stilling,” “restraint” and “cessation” emphasize different aspects of this demanding aim.

**My assessment:** My practical reading begins with freedom from being driven by every thought. That is an accessible starting point, not a claim that ordinary calm exhausts the classical meaning.

## Study index 1.3

**Reading:** When mental activity is stilled, the seer stands in its own nature. This presupposes a distinction between awareness and changing mental contents.

**My assessment:** The distinction is philosophically fertile. Noticing a thought and being identical with it are different propositions; that observation alone, however, does not prove an eternal, separate self.
