# Do these seven commentators explain all 196 sutras?

15 September 2026 · Research update 0.2

**They are not seven equivalent sets of 196 separate explanations.** Six have identifiable commentary works covering the Yoga Sutras as a whole. A complete numbered Sidersky commentary has not been verified. The other editions also differ in numbering, and a translation at a given number does not necessarily have its own prose explanation.

## Vyāsa source quality

Jha’s 1907 translation is a historical public-domain work. Its title also identifies notes drawn from other commentators. The collected blocks retain those notes under their source labels: they must not all be described as Vyāsa’s own words.

The main Harvard/Google OCR lacks printed page 86, affecting the end of 2.34 and entries 2.35–2.37. That span was restored from an [independent Kerala scan of the same work](https://archive.org/details/pli.kerala.rare.28327). Damaged heading tokens were corrected to index the complete sequence. Ordinary OCR errors remain in the prose and Sanskrit transliterations; this material needs proofreading before quotation or publication.

The available Rāma Prasāda alternatives were extracted only from the section explicitly headed as Vyāsa’s commentary, ending before Vācaspati Miśra’s gloss. Some source pages discuss more than one sutra; the structured data retains that shared coverage. The complete Rāma Prasāda edition has not been imported.

## How to finish the modern commentary layers

For each modern author, select an exact edition and work from verified passages. We can write concise, source-grounded comparison notes and attach page references, with permission or licensed content where substantial verbatim publication is intended. Uploaded excerpts can also be analyzed and compared.

For Sidersky, the missing evidence is an identifiable complete book, manuscript or complete numbered lecture series. Until one is supplied or verified, his contribution remains a valuable thematic perspective.
