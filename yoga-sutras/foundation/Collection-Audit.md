# Audit of the Yoga Sutras research collection

Version 0.3 · 15 September 2026

## Overall result

**Usable as a research reader and editorial draft; not ready as a fully verified comparative book.** All 196 study entries now have a short main explanation and a separate original AI commentary. Existing historical translations and the seven selected commentator layers are preserved. The largest remaining gaps are unread or unavailable modern commentary, unproofread historical OCR, and edition-specific mappings that remain provisional.

## What this audit actually covered

The audit checks every structured sutra record, all 585 historical translations for presence and source-number coverage, all 1,372 selected-author slots, the complete reader, and the combined manuscript. It compares stored Sanskrit with the retrieved GRETIL transcription and reproduces the Devanagari conversion. All new main explanations and original comments were reviewed during composition against the collected Sanskrit and English translations.

This is **not** a complete line-by-line philological collation, independent expert review, proofread edition of the Jha scan, verification of spiritual attainments, or an audit of the full modern books. Source URLs identify provenance; this run does not certify that every external link is currently live. Main explanations synthesize the available Sanskrit and historical translation layer; they do not purport to incorporate every view in all seven authors’ books.

## Findings that remain open

### High priority before publication

1. **Incomplete comparative coverage.** Empty modern-author slots remain empty. Sidersky is represented by thematic interview connections, not a verified commentary on all sutras. Do not advertise seven complete commentaries or a synthesis of all their views.
2. **Jha OCR quality.** All 195 included blocks remain unproofread. Examples such as “Samddki,” damaged names, page headers and split words survive. Retain the raw evidence, but proofread each block against page images before treating it as dependable reading text. The supplied blocks include Jha’s own labeled Notes and material drawing on Vācaspati Miśra and Vijñānabhikṣu; attributing the entire block to Vyāsa would be inaccurate.
3. **Edition-level verification.** The 196/195 distinctions are represented, but exact mappings in the selected modern editions and paragraph coverage still need checking. A complete book and a separate prose comment on every numbered sutra are different claims.
4. **Reuse conditions.** The GRETIL e-text and generated Devanagari carry the recorded CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 terms. Modern copyrighted books are not included in full. Keep source-specific rights and attribution with the content, and resolve the intended publication use before distributing a final book or commercial app.

### Specific passages requiring attention

- **Study 1.47 — translation divergence:** The included Vivekananda translation emphasizes a firmly fixed mind; GRETIL adhyātmaprasāda and the Woods and Johnston renderings emphasize inward clarity or serenity. Preserve the distinction; this audit does not diagnose it as an OCR error.
- **Study 2.20 — transcription issue:** Woods’s online transcription prints “Buddha” after “undefiled,” where the Sanskrit reads śuddha. Retained as received and flagged for comparison with the printed edition.
- **Study 2.25 — translation divergence:** The included Vivekananda wording calls the absence of junction “the thing-to-be-avoided”; the Sanskrit hānaṃ and the Woods and Johnston translations describe cessation or release. Potentially misleading wording is preserved pending an edition-level check.
- **Study 2.50 — translation divergence:** The included Vivekananda translation says “long or short”; GRETIL dīrghasūkṣmaḥ and the other collected translations give long/protracted and subtle. Keep the source wording distinct from the plain-English explanation.
- **Study 3.19 — interpretive expansion:** Vivekananda’s translation specifies signs in another person’s body. This explanatory addition is not explicit in the brief GRETIL Sanskrit pratyayasya paracittajñānam; Woods and Johnston refer to the mental presentation. It is not silently removed.
- **Study 3.22 — numbering exception:** No separately numbered entry in the GRETIL, Woods, Johnston or Jha sequences used here. This study entry uses Vivekananda source 3.22. Subsequent chapter-three source references must retain the offset.
- **Study 3.31 — translation omission:** The included Vivekananda translation names hunger only; GRETIL source 3.30, kṣutpipāsā, and Woods and Johnston source 3.30 name hunger and thirst. The main explanation includes both; the historical translation remains unchanged.
- **Study 3.56 — interpretive difference:** GRETIL source 3.55 speaks of equality of purity between sattva and puruṣa. The relation of this wording to distinction or union must be explained by author; the collection must not manufacture agreement among different philosophical readings.
- **Study 4.16 — numbering exception:** Absent from Vivekananda’s numbered sequence and discussed in a collected chapter-four editorial footnote. Subsequent Vivekananda source references are one lower than the study index.
- **Study 4.26 — textual variant:** Vivekananda source 4.25 describes a “previous state” of kaivalya; the collected chapter-four editor’s footnote records an alternative reading meaning inclination toward kaivalya. The GRETIL text uses that latter reading. Preserve both with their attribution.

These are flags, not a claim that every difference is an error. Except for the new explanatory notes and presentation, historical text has not been silently altered in this update.

## Interpretation review

The new writing distinguishes descriptive readings from the AI editor’s assessment. It retains the text’s devotional, ascetic and metaphysical claims rather than quietly replacing them with modern psychology. Chapter three’s powers are described as claims; contemporary analogies are not offered as proof. Difficult ethical passages are discussed directly, including 2.12–2.14, 2.35–2.40 and 4.7. Different meanings of liberation, purity and union are not combined into a false consensus.

This review remains an AI editorial draft. A Sanskrit specialist and a human editor should assess the final prose, especially compressed or disputed passages such as 1.19, 2.27, 2.47, 2.51, 3.36, 3.44, 3.56 and 4.22. The number of flagged passages is not an estimate of every remaining problem.
