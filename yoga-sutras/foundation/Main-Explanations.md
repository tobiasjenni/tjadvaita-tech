## Study index 1.1

**अथ योगानुशासनम् ॥**

*atha yogānuśāsanam*

The teaching of yoga begins here. It invites us into a disciplined exploration of the mind, rather than offering a single technique or a quick result.

## Study index 1.2

**योगश् चित्तवृत्तिनिरोधः ॥**

*yogaś cittavṛttinirodhaḥ*

Yoga aims to still the mind’s changing activity so that awareness is no longer caught up in it. Commentators differ over how to explain this stilling and its final depth.

[GRETIL / Āgāśe 1904 1.2](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_pataJjali-yogasUtra.htm) · [Swami Vivekananda 1.2](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Complete_Works_of_Swami_Vivekananda/Volume_1/Raja-Yoga/Patanjali%27s_Yoga_Aphorisms_-_Concentration%3A_Its_Spiritual_Uses) · [James Haughton Woods 1.2](https://www.amsi.ge/pat/ys_woods.html) · [Charles Johnston 1.2](https://theosophylib.com/cj/yoga-sutras-1909/)

## Study index 1.3

**तदा द्रष्टुः स्वरूपे ऽवस्थानम् ॥**

*tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe 'vasthānam*

When mental activity is stilled, the “seer,” or conscious awareness, rests in its own nature. Classical Yoga distinguishes this awareness from the thoughts and feelings that pass through the mind.

## Study index 1.4

**वृत्तिसारूप्यम् इतरत्र ॥**

*vṛttisārūpyam itaratra*

At other times, the seer appears identified with the mind’s changing forms. We experience a thought, emotion or perception as though it defined who we are.

## Study index 1.5

**वृत्तयः पञ्चतय्यः क्लिष्टाक्लिष्टाः ॥**

*vṛttayaḥ pañcatayyaḥ kliṣṭākliṣṭāḥ*

Mental activities fall into five groups and can either sustain affliction or help weaken it. The aim is to understand their role, rather than assume every thought is equally harmful.

## Study index 1.6

*pramāṇaviparyayavikalpanidrāsmṛtayaḥ*

The five kinds of mental activity are valid knowledge, mistaken knowledge, conceptual construction, sleep and memory. The following sutras explain each category.

## Study index 1.7

*pratyakṣānumānāgamāḥ pramāṇāni*

Reliable knowledge comes through perception, inference and trustworthy testimony. These are distinct ways of knowing, each requiring care about what it can actually establish.

## Study index 1.8

*anubhūtaviṣayāsaṃpramoṣaḥ smṛtiḥ*

## Study index 1.26

**पूर्वेषाम् अपि गुरुः कालेनानवच्छेदात् ॥**
