# Yoga Sutras: research foundation for the book and app

15 September 2026 · Research collection, version 0.2

## What is ready

The collection contains **all 196 entries in a combined Sanskrit index**, with Devanagari and IAST transliteration, and **three historical English translations** for comparison. Each of the three English sources has 195 numbered passages; the passages they omit differ. Every entry in this collection has Sanskrit and at least one English translation.

It also contains the collected online prose commentaries by **Swami Vivekananda** and **Charles Johnston**, including Johnston’s four introductions. These are historical perspectives to consult, not a decision about which commentators our final book will endorse. **The selected commentators are Sidersky, Vyāsa, Taimni, Vivekananda, Satchidananda, B. K. S. Iyengar and Edwin F. Bryant.** See the [coverage report](Commentator-Coverage-Report.md) for what each work covers and what has actually been added.

This is a substantial starting corpus, not an exhaustive library of everything written about the Yoga Sutras or a finished critical edition.

## What the text covers

These are editorial orientation notes; the English chapter labels are descriptive rather than uniquely correct translations.

| 3. Vibhūtipāda — accomplishments and powers | Concentration, meditation and absorption together; transformation, extraordinary capacities and detachment from them | 3.1–3.8; 3.16 onward; 3.38; 3.50–3.56 |
| 4. Kaivalyapāda — liberation / independence | Mind, objects, impressions, discernment and the culmination of liberation | 4.7–4.11; 4.15–4.24; 4.25–4.34 |

The eight limbs at **2.29** are yama, niyama, āsana, prāṇāyāma, pratyāhāra, dhāraṇā, dhyāna and samādhi. The final three are defined at **3.1–3.3**; their combined application is called **saṃyama at 3.4**. Saṃyama should therefore be linked to those three rather than automatically displayed as an additional classical limb. [Sanskrit source](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_pataJjali-yogasUtra.htm).

## How to compare the English versions

- **Vivekananda:** an influential practical exposition in *Raja-Yoga*, with his own philosophical explanations. His English and commentary are distinct from the Sanskrit base text.
- **Woods:** a technical historical translation. Expressions such as “mind-stuff,” “constraint,” and “Isolation” need explanation for a contemporary reader. His full book also translates classical commentaries; this package contains only its sutra-translation excerpt. [Woods source](https://www.amsi.ge/pat/ys_woods.html).
- **Johnston:** an explicitly spiritual and Theosophical reading, with Christian comparisons. This provides a useful example of how strongly interpretation can shape translation. [Johnston source](https://theosophylib.com/cj/yoga-sutras-1909/).

For example, at **1.2**, the translators use different language for *yoga*, *citta*, *vṛtti* and *nirodha*. At **4.34**, compare their accounts of liberation with the Sanskrit terms *puruṣa*, *guṇa*, *kaivalya* and *citiśakti*. Do not smooth those differences into apparent agreement.

Our eventual plain-English explanation can sit beside these sources and identify where it makes an interpretive choice.

## Foundation still to develop

1. **A classical commentary layer.** Read Vyāsa’s *Bhāṣya* alongside the sutras, then selected Vācaspati Miśra explanations. The [Rāma Prasāda edition](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutras-with-commentaries) is a located English access point. Its full commentary has not been imported into this package.
2. **Your selected modern commentators.** The authors have now been selected; verify each exact edition and passage location. Keep an author’s written translation separate from an interview summary.
3. **Sanskrit review.** Choose a publication base text, compare variant readings, check word division and create a word-by-word analysis where it helps readers.
4. **A contemporary English editorial layer.** Decide tone and audience, then draft explanations with explicit source references. Historical translations alone will be difficult for many beginners.
5. **Russian and German editions.** Translate against the Sanskrit and agreed editorial meaning, keeping the source of each commentary visible. Maintain a multilingual terminology list rather than relying only on translations of English paraphrases.

For historical and textual research, [Gerald James Larson’s introduction](https://geraldjameslarson.com/pdf/PrefaceandIntroduction_1-35.pdf) is a further reading lead. Dating and the relationships between the sutras and their commentaries deserve careful treatment; this draft does not assert a single settled date or biographical account of Patañjali.

## One content foundation for both formats

Each sutra can have: stable ID → Sanskrit → source-specific translations → named commentaries → our synthesis → optional reflection or practice → references.

The **book** can present a readable selected path through these layers. The **app** can let readers switch language and commentator, compare terms and return to saved notes. The JSON supplied here is an initial content source for that shared structure; it is not an implemented app.

Before publication, resolve the GRETIL e-text’s **noncommercial license** and complete textual proofreading. See the [full source and reuse notes](Sources-and-Editorial-Notes.md).
